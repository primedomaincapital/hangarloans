Hangar financing, FBO growth, and the business jet fleet, by the numbers

Business jet deliveries, business aviation departures, FBO market size, hangar construction costs, aircraft loan rates and terms, and state hangar loan programs, from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures demand: the jets being delivered and the departures being flown, which decide how many hangars are needed. The second kind measures the building: what a hangar costs to put up, and what the loan that pays for it looks like. The third kind measures the service business around the building: the fixed-base operators whose fuel, hangarage, and handling revenue depends on that same roof space. The first kind tells a reader how fast the need is growing. The second tells a reader what each unit of it costs. The third tells a reader who earns from it.

What the latest numbers show is demand outrunning the roof: business jet deliveries rose 11.8% in 2025 to their strongest level since 2009, departures set a record at 3.9 million, and the FBO market is projected to grow by roughly half by the early 2030s — while a hangar still costs $60 to $120 per square foot to build and the lenders who understand that building remain few.

How to read these numbers

Included: GAMA shipment and billing figures for 2025 as reported in February 2026; McKinsey's 2026 state-of-business-aviation figures for departures, hours, and the fractional fleet; FBO market sizes from two publishers with their scopes stated wherever they disagree; hangar construction cost ranges from three 2025–2026 industry guides; the Nebraska and California state hangar-loan programs as published; AOPA Finance loan terms and rates as published; and FAA general-aviation fleet figures. Excluded: any single FBO market-size headline presented without its scope or publisher; undated practitioner cost claims presented as dated figures; and any hangar-rental rate, which no published source verified to standard for this page.

Published FBO market-size estimates differ by scope — some count fueling, hangarage, maintenance, and passenger services together, others count a narrower service set — so the range is shown wherever publishers disagree, and no single figure is presented as the only measure. Forecasts are labeled as forecasts, not actuals. Hangar construction costs are ranges by finish level and state, not quotes; site work, doors, and utilities are separate costs in every guide cited.
