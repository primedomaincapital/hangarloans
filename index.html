The thesis

A name for the bottleneck in private aviation.

Business aviation just recorded its busiest year ever — 3.9 million departures in 2025, with flight hours up more than a third since 2019. Every one of those aircraft needs somewhere to sit between flights, and at the leisure airports where growth is fastest, hangar space is the constraint that decides whether an airport can take more traffic at all.

That is the gap this name sits in. Hangar financing is a documented niche with a shortage of lenders: the aircraft-finance houses that fund the jet will not fund the building, and the commercial banks that fund buildings do not understand ground leases, airport authorities, or the federal obligations that come with airfield land. A borrower who needs a hangar loan has to find one of the few lenders who knows the difference — and that lender has to be found first.

The name does the finding. "Hangar loans" and "hangar financing" are the words owners, developers, and airport managers actually type when they need this product, and this domain is those words, in order, with nothing added. It works on a lender's letterhead, on an FBO developer's proposal, and on a quote page where a borrower decides in three seconds whether the firm in front of them knows this market.

Exact, and built for a capitalized buyer pool. A hangar is not a small purchase: a 15,000-square-foot facility runs $900,000 to $1,800,000 to build, and larger jet facilities run past $2 million. The firms that finance, build, and operate those buildings are capitalized, and they buy names that match the product they sell. This one matches it exactly.
